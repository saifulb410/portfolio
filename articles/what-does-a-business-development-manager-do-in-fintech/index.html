What Does a Business Development Manager Do in Fintech?

By Saiful Islam, Business Development Manager at WeMasterTrade and founder of WeTrainEducation & Tech

When people hear "business development", they usually think of sales calls. In fintech and online trading, the job is wider. It is about opening a market, building the partners who bring customers to you, and making sure the operations behind them actually work. This is how I see the role, based on my work developing the Bangladesh market at WeMasterTrade.

1. Opening and developing a market

Entering a new country is not only about translating a website. A business development manager studies how local customers find, trust and pay for a product, then adapts marketing, support and payment options to that reality. In Bangladesh, for example, local payment methods, local-language community channels such as Telegram and Discord, and trusted local voices matter as much as the product itself.

2. Building partners and affiliates

Partnerships and affiliate programs are often the main growth engine. The work includes finding the right partners, explaining the offer clearly, agreeing fair terms, and then supporting them after they sign. A partner who feels ignored after onboarding rarely stays active, so ongoing communication and fast answers to their questions matter as much as the first deal.

3. Working with influencers and communities

Influencers and community leaders bring credibility that advertising cannot buy. Good influencer work means choosing people whose audience actually fits the product, being honest about what the product does, and keeping a long-term relationship rather than a one-off post. Communities on Telegram, Discord and social media then turn new users into regulars through events, webinars and quick support.

4. Keeping payments and support running

In fintech, growth that the operations cannot support is a problem. Customers need payments to go through, accounts to work and questions to be answered quickly. A business development manager stays close to payment operations and customer support, because every failed payment or slow answer undoes the effort spent on acquiring that customer.

5. Feeding the product team

Because business development talks to partners and customers daily, it sees problems early: a missing feature, a confusing step, a payment option people ask for. Passing that feedback to the product and IT teams, with clear examples, is one of the most useful things the role does.

6. Measuring what matters

Reporting keeps the work honest. Useful measures include:

  • New sign-ups and how many become active customers
  • Performance of each partner, affiliate or influencer
  • Cost of acquiring a customer compared with the value they bring
  • Customer retention and support response times
  • Payment success rates and common failure reasons

Reviewing these regularly shows which partners to invest in, which channels to drop, and where operations need attention.

Skills that help

Communication and negotiation are essential, but so is a technical understanding of how payment systems, CRMs and support tools work. That is why I pair business development with a Computer Science & Engineering background: it makes conversations with product and IT teams faster and more precise.

Final thought

Business development in fintech is a mix of growth, relationships and operations. The people who do it well are curious about the product, patient with partners, and disciplined about the numbers.
